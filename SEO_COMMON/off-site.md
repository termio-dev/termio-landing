# Off-site SEO

Backlinks (other sites linking to yours) and brand mentions. The hardest
part of SEO because it's mostly outreach and content marketing, not code.

## Why off-site matters more than on-page

A perfectly-built site with zero backlinks loses to a mediocre site with 50
quality backlinks. Backlinks are still Google's strongest authority signal,
and the only one competitors can't trivially copy.

For a brand-new site, expect:
- **Months 1–3**: pure on-page work doesn't move rankings much. You're
  building the foundation but not yet trusted by Google.
- **Months 3–6**: first backlinks land. Some long-tail rankings appear.
- **Months 6–12**: you either accumulate links (and rankings climb) or you
  stagnate.

Allocate at least as much time to off-site as on-site for the first year.

## Backlink quality > quantity

One link from a high-authority site (newspaper, government, university) is
worth hundreds of links from low-quality directories. Quality dimensions:

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Domain authority** of linking site | Authority flows downward |
| **Topical relevance** | A car-wash blog linking to a car-wash directory >> a recipe blog linking to it |
| **Editorial vs. paid** | Editorial links rank far better |
| **Link position** (in body vs. footer) | Body content links pass more equity |
| **Anchor text** | Descriptive anchors that match your keywords help — but don't over-optimize (looks spammy) |
| **dofollow vs. nofollow** | dofollow passes equity; nofollow still helps for brand signals |

## Tier 1: free, do-on-launch (1 evening)

Every new site should land these within the first week.

- **Local business directories** for your country/city. Examples for Moldova:
  `infoMD.md`, `9111.md`, `999.md`, `allmoldova.com`, `yellowpages.md`,
  `gid.md`. Each takes ~10 min, gives you a .md or .com domain backlink.
- **Google Business Profile** (formerly Google My Business). Doesn't pass
  link equity but anchors brand entity for Google's knowledge graph.
- **Bing Places**. Same as Google Business but for Bing. Free, takes 5 min.
- **Apple Business Connect** if you serve iOS users.
- **OpenStreetMap (OSM)**: if your domain references real-world places, add
  your URL as the `website` tag on relevant OSM nodes. Some scrapers and
  Wikipedia tools cite OSM, surfacing your URL.
- **Wikidata**: if your business has a Wikipedia page (probably not for new
  sites), add the URL there.
- **Industry-specific directories**. A 20-minute Google search for
  `"<your industry>" directory` will surface 5–10 worth applying to.
- **Cross-link from any other domain you own** (personal site, side
  projects). Free authority transfer.

Time investment: 1 evening. Yields 10–20 backlinks from real domains.

## Tier 2: medium effort (ongoing)

- **Industry forums and Reddit**: be genuinely helpful in threads asking
  questions your site can answer. Link your site only when it actually
  answers the question. Spam links get removed and damage reputation.
- **Local subreddits and Facebook groups**: same — be useful first.
- **Comment on industry blogs** with substantive comments (not "great
  post"). Some allow a URL.
- **Guest posts**: write a quality piece for an established blog in your
  niche, with a single contextual link to your site in the author bio or
  body. Hardest to get but most valuable.
- **Help-A-Reporter (HARO) / Qwoted**: journalists request expert quotes
  daily. Reply with a useful quote, often get cited with a link.
- **Press releases** for genuine news (launch, milestone, new dataset).
  Avoid generic PR distribution services — pitch directly to relevant local
  journalists.

## Tier 3: lead-magnet content (the multiplier)

The single highest-ROI off-site strategy: publish data, tools, or analyses
that other people *want to cite*. They link organically, which compounds.

Examples:
- **Data summaries no competitor publishes**: "Average price of X in each
  district of [city], 2026". Pulled from your own dataset, formatted as a
  clean table.
- **Free tools** related to your industry: calculator, lookup, comparison.
- **Industry reports**: annual analysis, trend posts.
- **Curated lists**: "Best X for Y", "X options compared".

Each of these is a single page that can attract dozens of backlinks over
years. Worth more than 100 directory submissions.

## Local SEO (citations)

If your business serves a geographic area, **NAP consistency** (Name,
Address, Phone) across the web is critical. Same exact spelling and
formatting everywhere.

Tools to find and fix inconsistent listings:
- BrightLocal, Whitespark, Moz Local (paid).
- Manual: Google your business name + address, check first 5 pages for
  outdated/wrong info.

Common NAP mistakes:
- Phone format varies (`+373 22 ...` vs. `022 ...` vs. `(022) ...`).
- Old address still listed on directories you forgot.
- Suite/floor number sometimes included, sometimes not.

## Brand signals (mentions without links)

Google can detect brand mentions (your name appearing in text) even without
a hyperlink. These contribute to brand authority. Reach out and politely ask
for a link when you find unlinked mentions:

- Set up Google Alerts for your brand name.
- Quarterly: search `"Your Brand Name" -site:yourdomain.com` and look
  through results.

## What NOT to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| **Buy links** from PBN networks | Google identifies these and applies manual penalties. Recovery takes years. |
| **Link exchanges** ("you link me, I'll link you") at scale | Pattern-detected, devalued. Small amount is fine; spreadsheet-driven exchanges aren't. |
| **Comment-spam blogs and forums** | Wastes time, damages reputation, links get removed. |
| **Submit to 1000s of low-quality directories** | The "submit-everywhere" services. Google ignores or penalizes these. |
| **Press release distribution at scale** | Modern Google ignores boilerplate PR sites. Direct outreach beats this. |
| **Buy expired domains for backlinks** (PBN) | Recognized pattern. Risky. |
| **"Reciprocal" links from unrelated sites** | Recipes site linking to car wash site = obvious manipulation. |
| **Hidden links** (white text, 1px font) | Manual penalty if caught. |

## Measuring backlink growth

Tools (free tier exists for all):
- **Google Search Console**: shows your most-linked pages and top linking
  domains. Free. Should be your first stop.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools**: more detail on referring domains, anchor text.
  Free for verified sites.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools**: similar, separate index.
- **Moz Link Explorer**: limited free queries.

Track over time:
- **Referring domains** (count of unique domains linking to you) — most
  important number.
- **Total backlinks** (less important — one site can link many times).
- **New vs. lost links** weekly.

Realistic targets for a new site:
- Month 1: 5–10 referring domains (from Tier 1 directories).
- Month 3: 15–25.
- Month 6: 30–50.
- Month 12: 60–100+ if you're publishing lead-magnet content.

## When to skip off-site work

Off-site is the slowest lever. Skip it short-term if:
- **You don't yet have working on-page basics.** Backlinks to a broken site
  waste effort. Fix on-page first.
- **You haven't validated the business.** Don't spend months building
  authority for a site that might pivot.
- **You can't measure conversions.** SEO traffic without analytics is
  invisible — set up Search Console and analytics first.

## The audit "0 backlinks" complaint

Every new-site SEO audit will flag "weak backlink profile" or "0 referring
domains". This is descriptive, not actionable from code:

- Acknowledge: "yes, the site is new."
- Plan: pick from Tier 1 above and execute once a week for the first month.
- Don't waste time: no code change can manufacture backlinks. Move on to
  fixable on-page items in the same audit.
